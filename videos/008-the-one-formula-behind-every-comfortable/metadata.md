**Title:** The One Formula Behind Every Comfortable Staircase
**Alternates:** Why Some Stairs Make You Stumble · Every Good Staircase Obeys 2R + T = 63 cm
**Playlist:** math-for-engineers
**Description:** Why do some stairs feel effortless, while others make you shuffle, stretch or climb on your toes? The answer is one line of algebra written down in the late 1600s by the French architect François Blondel: twice the rise plus the tread should match one easy step, about 63 cm (25 inches).

You'll see where the formula comes from, test it on a comfortable stair, a set of garden steps and a steep attic stair, and plot every possible stair on one straight line. Then we compare it with real building codes (US IRC and IBC, UK Approved Document K) and design a full staircase from scratch: number of risers, tread depth, total length and angle.

Assumptions: a comfortable stride on a stair of 2R + T ≈ 63 cm (designers use roughly 60–65 cm); code values are the US IRC 2021 / IBC 2021 and UK Approved Document K limits. Always check your local code before building.
