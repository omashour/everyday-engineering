**Title:** The 63-Centimeter Rule Behind Comfortable Stairs
**Alternates:** Why Some Stairs Feel Awkward: The 2R + T Rule · Blondel's Stair Rule, Explained With Algebra
**Playlist:** math-for-engineers
**Description:** Why do some stairs feel easy while others feel awkward? Much of the answer comes down to two measurements, the rise and the tread depth, and a rule of thumb written down in the late 1600s by the French architect François Blondel: twice the rise plus the tread depth should land near one easy step.

You'll see why the "2" makes the rule a trade-off (+1 cm of rise means −2 cm of tread), test it on a comfortable stair, a set of garden steps and a steep attic stair, and see why two stairs with the same score can have very different pitches. Then we look at the US model codes (IRC and IBC), England's Approved Document K, a lab study on a step just 10 mm too tall, and design a straight flight from scratch.

Assumptions: a target of 2R + T = 63 cm for the worked examples (Blondel's own target was about 65 cm; real step lengths vary). US limits are model-code provisions, adopted and sometimes amended locally. The design example checks two dimensions only; a real stair also needs landings, headroom and handrails. Always check your local code.
