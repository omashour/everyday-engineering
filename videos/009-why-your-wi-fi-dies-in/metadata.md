**Title:** Why Your Wi-Fi Dies in One Room
**Alternates:** Twice as Far, a Quarter of the Wi-Fi Signal · The Inverse Square Law in Your Living Room
**Playlist:** math-for-engineers
**Description:** Your Wi-Fi is perfect next to the router and almost dead in one room. In open space, move twice as far away and you get a quarter of the signal. But inside a home, that's only half the story.

You'll see where the inverse square law comes from (power spreading over a sphere of area 4πr²), why every doubling of distance costs about 6 dB, what the dBm numbers on your phone mean, and why walls often cost as much signal as distance does. Then we add up a real path, distance plus walls, and see what moving the router does.

Assumptions: a reading of −30 dBm at 1 m and open-space (inverse square) spreading; typical 2.4 GHz wall losses of about 4 dB for drywall, 8 dB for brick and 10–15 dB for concrete (real walls vary). Signal-strength guidance is a rough industry rule of thumb.
