**Title:** Why Your Wi-Fi Dies in One Room
**Alternates:** Twice as Far, a Quarter of the Wi-Fi Power · The Inverse Square Law in Your Living Room
**Playlist:** math-for-engineers
**Description:** Your Wi-Fi is perfect next to the router and almost dead in one room. In ideal open space, move twice as far away and you receive a quarter of the power. But inside a home, that's only half the story.

You'll see where the inverse square law comes from (power spreading over a sphere of area 4πr²), why every doubling of distance costs about 6 dB, what the dBm numbers on your phone roughly mean, and why walls can cost as much signal as distance, or much more. Then we add up a simplified example, distance plus walls, and see what moving the router does in the same model.

Assumptions: a reading of −30 dBm at 1 m; ideal open-space (inverse square) spreading with the same antennas and frequency; example wall losses of 4 dB per drywall wall and 22 dB for one thick concrete wall (illustrative values; real walls vary widely). Real indoor signals also fluctuate because of reflections. Signal-strength guidance is a rough rule of thumb, not hard cutoffs.
