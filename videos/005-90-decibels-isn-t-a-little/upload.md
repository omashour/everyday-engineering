## Description (1279 characters)
```text
A machine at 90 decibels sounds only a little noisier than one at 80. But it has ten times the sound intensity, so over the same time ten times as much sound energy reaches your ears. That changes how long you should work next to it.

We build the decibel formula L = 10 log₁₀(I/I₀), see why every +10 dB is ×10 and every +3 dB is about ×2, why two independent 80 dB machines make about 83 dB (not 160), why a 10 dB increase often sounds only about twice as loud, and how NIOSH and OSHA turn this math into daily noise exposure limits.

Assumptions: sound intensity levels relative to I₀ = 10⁻¹² W/m²; typical levels are approximate (CDC) and depend on source and distance; workplace limits are A-weighted 8-hour averages and are guidelines, not guarantees for every individual.
```
