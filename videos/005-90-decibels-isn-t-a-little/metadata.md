**Title:** 90 Decibels Isn't a Bit More Than 80. It's 10x the Energy
**Alternates:** Why +10 Decibels Means 10x the Sound Energy · The Logarithm Hiding in Every Noise Meter

**Description:**
A machine at 90 decibels sounds only a little noisier than one at 80. But it carries ten times the sound energy, and that changes how long you can safely work next to it.

We build the decibel formula L = 10 log₁₀(I/I₀), see why every +10 dB is ×10 and every +3 dB is ×2, why two 80 dB machines make 83 dB (not 160), why your ears hear +10 dB as only about twice as loud, and how NIOSH and OSHA turn this math into daily noise limits.

Assumptions: sound intensity levels relative to I₀ = 10⁻¹² W/m²; typical sound levels from the CDC; workplace limits are A-weighted 8-hour averages.
