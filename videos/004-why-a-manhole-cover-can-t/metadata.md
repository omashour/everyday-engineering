**Title:** Why a Manhole Cover Can't Fall Into Its Hole
**Alternates:** Why Manhole Covers Are Round · The Shape That Can't Fall Through Its Own Hole
**Playlist:** math-for-engineers
**Description:**
A thin square cover can slip through its own hole if you tilt and turn it. A rigid round cover over a smaller round opening never can. The reason is one line of geometry.

We look at how a cover rests on its lip, why a square can pass along its diagonal (s√2 ≈ 1.41 s), and what "constant width" means. Then we build the Reuleaux triangle, a curved triangle with constant width and about 10.3% less area than a circle of the same width, and see constant width in real life, from British 20p and 50p coins to rollers that aren't round.

Assumptions: example cover 60 cm wide with a 2 cm lip; shapes drawn as ideal geometric figures. Real covers also depend on thickness, frames and fittings.
