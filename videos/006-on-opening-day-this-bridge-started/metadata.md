**Title:** On Opening Day, This Bridge Started Swaying
**Alternates:** Why London's Wobbly Bridge Closed After 2 Days · The Eigenvalue That Shut Down a London Bridge
**Playlist:** why-the-world-works
**Description:** In June 2000, London opened the Millennium Bridge. Two days later it was closed: the deck was swaying so badly that people grabbed the handrails to stay upright.

You'll learn what a natural frequency is, why natural frequencies are eigenvalues of a structure's stiffness and mass, how two masses on springs give exactly two frequencies and two mode shapes, why walking pushes sideways at about 1 Hz, and how resonance plus a crowd feedback loop made the bridge wobble. Then you'll see how engineers fixed it with damping.

Assumptions: the two-mass example and the resonance graph (5% and 20% damping, natural frequency 1 Hz) are simplified illustrations, not the bridge's measured values. Bridge facts come from published engineering accounts of the Millennium Bridge.

**Pinned comment:** Quick question: you take about 2 steps per second, so why does walking push a bridge sideways at only about 1 Hz? Answer: one full left-right sway takes two steps (left foot, then right foot), so the sideways push repeats at half your step rate, about 1 Hz.
