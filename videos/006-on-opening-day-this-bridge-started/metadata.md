**Title:** On Opening Day, This Bridge Started Swaying
**Alternates:** Why London's Wobbly Bridge Closed After 2 Days · The Eigenvalues Behind London's Wobbly Bridge
**Playlist:** why-the-world-works
**Description:** On 10 June 2000, London opened the Millennium Bridge. The deck began swaying sideways, people grabbed the railings to keep their balance, and two days later the bridge closed.

You'll learn what natural frequencies and mode shapes are, how a two-mass model turns into an eigenvalue problem (K v = ω² M v), why walking pushes sideways at about 1 Hz, and why resonance with walking can't be the whole story. Then you'll see the real reason the sway grew: an energy balance between the crowd and the bridge's damping, and how dampers tipped that balance.

Assumptions: the two-mass model uses identical masses and springs with no damping, and the resonance graph (5% and 20% damping, natural frequency 1 Hz) is a simplified example with a steady external force. Opening-day motion values are estimates from observations and video. Sources: Dallard et al. (2001), J. Bridge Eng. 6(6):412; Belykh et al. (2021), Nature Communications 12:7223.

**Pinned comment:** Quick question: you take about 2 steps per second, so why does walking push a bridge sideways at only about 1 Hz? Answer: one full left-right cycle takes two steps (left foot, then right foot), so the sideways force repeats at half your step rate, about 1 Hz.
