**Title:** Your Phone Doesn't Know Where You Are
**Alternates:** How GPS Finds You With Circles and Spheres · Why GPS Needs Four Satellites, Not Three
**Playlist:** math-for-engineers
**Description:** Your phone doesn't start out knowing where you are. It starts with nothing but timings: how long signals took to arrive from satellites about 20,000 km up. From those, it finds you to within a few meters.

You'll see how a travel time becomes a distance, why one distance puts you on a circle, how subtracting two circle equations turns them into a straight line, and how a third distance picks a single point (trilateration). Then we move to three dimensions and meet the catch: a clock error of one microsecond shifts every distance by about 300 m. That's why a GPS receiver needs at least four satellites.

Assumptions: the map example is a simplified flat model with exact distances; real GPS works in three dimensions and also has to deal with atmospheric delays, signal reflections and satellite geometry. The accuracy figure (about 5 m) is typical for smartphones under open sky.
