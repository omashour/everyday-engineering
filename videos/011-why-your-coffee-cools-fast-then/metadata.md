**Title:** Why Your Coffee Cools Fast, Then Slow
**Alternates:** Milk Now or Later? The Math of Cooling Coffee · Newton's Law of Cooling in Your Coffee Mug
**Playlist:** math-for-engineers
**Description:** Your coffee doesn't cool at a steady speed. It loses heat fastest while it's hottest, then slower and slower. One law of cooling explains why, tells you when your coffee is drinkable, and settles whether to add the milk now or later.

You'll see Newton's law of cooling, why it leads to an exponential, how the gap to room temperature halves at a steady interval, how to use logs to find when coffee reaches 60 °C, and why cold milk added right away leaves your coffee slightly warmer ten minutes later. Then we look at where the same curve shows up in engineering.

Assumptions: coffee at 85 °C in a 20 °C room; k ≈ 0.07 per minute (the gap halves about every 10 minutes) for an open mug; milk is 20% of the drink at 5 °C, mixing as a simple weighted average, with the same k before and after mixing. Real cooling is only approximately exponential: evaporation, lids and mug shape all change it.
