**Title:** Why Your Coffee Cools Fast, Then Slow
**Alternates:** Milk Now or Later? The Math of Cooling Coffee · Newton's Law of Cooling in Your Coffee Mug
**Playlist:** math-for-engineers
**Description:** Your coffee doesn't cool at a steady speed. It cools fastest while it's hottest, then slower and slower. A simple model, Newton's law of cooling, explains why, estimates when your coffee reaches a temperature you like, and answers whether to add cold milk now or later.

You'll see why the model leads to an exponential, how the gap to room temperature halves at a steady interval, why the cooling curve flattens, how logs give the time to reach 60 °C, and why, under shared assumptions, cold milk added right away leaves the coffee slightly warmer ten minutes later. Then we look at where the same simple model shows up in engineering.

Assumptions: coffee at 85 °C in a room that stays at 20 °C; an assumed 10-minute half-life for the temperature gap (k = ln 2 / 10 ≈ 0.07 per minute); milk at 5 °C until poured, one fifth of the final drink, instant mixing as a simple weighted average, and the same half-life for both cups. Real cooling is only approximately exponential: evaporation, lids and the amount of liquid all matter.
