**Title:** Why Isn't a Soda Can as Wide as It Is Tall?
**Alternates:** The Calculus of the Perfect Soda Can · Calculus Says Your Soda Can Is the Wrong Shape. Is It?
**Playlist:** math-for-engineers
**Description:** Calculus says the can that uses the least metal should be exactly as wide as it is tall. A real soda can is almost twice as tall as it is wide. Is the can wrong, or is the math missing something?

You'll unroll a can into two circles and a rectangle, fix the volume at 355 mL, graph the metal against the width, and use one derivative to find the classic answer: h = 2r. Then we add what the textbook leaves out: the lid is about 2.4 times as thick as the wall. The same calculus then gives h/d = k, a taller can, and the real can turns out to sit right between the two answers, only about 2% above either minimum.

Assumptions: idealized cylinders holding 355 cm³ (a real can also has a neck, a domed bottom and headspace); wall ≈ 0.1 mm and lid ≈ 0.23 mm, with both ends assumed as thick as the lid in the second model.
