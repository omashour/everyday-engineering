## Description (1408 characters)
```text
A textbook model says a soda can should be as wide as it is tall. The can in your hand is almost twice as tall as it is wide. What changes when we model a real can?

You'll unroll a closed cylinder into two circles and a rectangle, fix the volume at 355 mL, graph the metal area against the diameter, and use one derivative to find the classic result: height equals diameter. Then we run a thought experiment: what if each end uses k times as much metal per square centimeter as the side? The optimum becomes h/D = k, a taller can, and the real can's diameter falls between the two modeled optima, only about 2% above either minimum.

Assumptions: idealized closed cylinders holding 355 cm³; uniform metal in the first model; flat, full-width ends weighted by an example value k = 2.4 in the second. A real can also has headspace, a neck and a domed base, so its actual metal use needs a different calculation.
```

## Pinned comment

```text
Try it: a closed cylinder with uniform metal must hold 400 cm³. What diameter uses the least metal? Answer: V = 2πr³, so r = (400/2π)^(1/3) ≈ 3.99 cm, and D = h ≈ 8.0 cm.
```
