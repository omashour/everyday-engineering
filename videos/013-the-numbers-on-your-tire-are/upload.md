## Description (1546 characters)
```text
Your speedometer says 60. In this example, you're actually doing about 64, and the difference starts with one digit on the tire. Many passenger-car tires carry a size code like 225/45R17, and a little math turns it into an estimate of the tire's outside diameter.

We read each part of the code, turn the aspect ratio into a sidewall height and estimate the outside diameter: about 634 mm, or just under 25 inches. Then we use the circumference to see how a speedometer and odometer turn wheel rotations into speed and distance, what happens when 45 becomes 55, and how larger rims with lower aspect ratios keep the diameter close to the original.

Assumptions: nominal, unloaded tire sizes; the speedometer was accurate on the original tires and its calibration is unchanged; distance per turn scales with nominal diameter. Real tires flex under load, so actual figures differ slightly. This is a geometry example, not fitment advice: always use a tire size approved by your vehicle manufacturer.
```

```text
Quick check: estimate the outside diameter of a 205/55R16 tire. Answer: sidewall height = 205 × 0.55 = 112.75 mm, rim = 16 × 25.4 = 406.4 mm, so D ≈ 406.4 + 2(112.75) = 631.9 mm ≈ 24.9 inches.
```
