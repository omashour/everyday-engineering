## Description (1512 characters)
```text
Two 12-inch pizzas or one 18-inch pizza: which gives you more pizza? The single 18-inch pizza has exactly 12.5% more surface area (81π vs 72π square inches).

The reason is one formula: area grows with the square of the diameter. We work out both areas, find the break-even diameter where one pizza equals two (12√2, about 16.97 inches), compare common pizza sizes and price per square inch, and see the one thing the pair wins: about a third more outer edge.

Then the same geometry in engineering: a pipe with twice the inside diameter has four times the opening area; for round conductors of the same material, length and temperature, doubling the conductor diameter cuts resistance to one-quarter; and a 3/4-inch bolt has 2.25 times the unthreaded shank area of a 1/2-inch bolt.

Assumptions: pizzas of similar thickness and topping coverage, so area is an estimate of how much food you get; prices are made-up examples; bolt comparison uses unthreaded shank areas (the threaded section has its own tensile stress area).
```
