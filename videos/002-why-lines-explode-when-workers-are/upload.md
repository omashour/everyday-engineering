## Description (1166 characters)
```text
A cashier who is busy 90% of the time gives an average wait of 9 minutes. Make her 95% busy and the wait jumps to 19. Just three more customers an hour doubles the line.

In this video you'll learn what utilization is, the queueing formula Wq = ρ/(1−ρ) × service time, why the spare capacity (1−ρ) on the bottom makes the curve shoot up like a hockey stick, and why randomness, not laziness, is what creates lines (Kingman's formula).

Assumptions: one server, random (Poisson) arrivals and random (exponential) service times with a 1-minute average. This is the classic M/M/1 queue. Real lines differ in detail, but the shape of the curve is the same.
```
