# Channel: Everyday Engineering

**Promise:** math, physics and engineering explained through the world around you. "Big ideas. Everyday examples."
**Audience:** high-school and college STEM students, future engineers, curious adults. Later: engineering students and working engineers (problem series, industrial engineering).
**Phases:** 1 math (now) → 2 physics & chemistry → 3 engineering fundamentals (solved problems, style F) → 4 industrial engineering.
**Look:** white whiteboard background, navy text/diagrams, yellow highlighter, red circles/arrows/answers, navy title and end cards. Faceless, narrated.

## Hook rules (first 10–15 seconds)
1. Open on the real object or situation, before any equation.
2. A surprising fact or question that makes viewers need the answer.
3. A promise: what they'll be able to do or understand by the end.
4. No logo, intro or greeting. Content from the first second.

Hook types to rotate: surprising number ("…doesn't double. It quadruples.") · everyday mystery ("Why is every dam thicker at the bottom?") · myth-buster ("You'll never use this? You used it this morning.") · real event ("In 2000, a London bridge started swaying…") · challenge ("Can you find the best can shape before I do?").

## Title formulas that work in this niche
- Surprising claim with a number: "Driving Twice as Fast Is 4 Times More Dangerous"
- "Why X …": "Why Every Dam Is Thicker at the Bottom"
- Hidden math in an everyday thing: "Your Electricity Bill Is an Integral"
- Story: "On Opening Day, This Bridge Started Swaying"
- Problem series: "Statics Problem 3: Beam with a Distributed Load"
Keep titles under ~60 characters, no clickbait the video doesn't pay off.

## Voice (narration is recorded with Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS)
Warm, clear, confident teacher talking to one curious student; energetic in the hook, calm in explanations. The local `tts-pack` command splits the narration into chunks and adds the style instructions, so `say` holds only the spoken words.

## Playlists (`video.playlist`)
- `math-for-engineers` · `why-the-world-works` · `engineering-problems` · `industrial-engineering`

## Thumbnails
White whiteboard look or a strong photo; ≤ 5 words; one highlighted word or number; one equation or diagram at most; readable at phone size. Always 2 variants (A/B test).

## Shorts
1–2 per video, 30–60 s, built from 2–4 scenes that make sense alone (usually the hook + the key result). Banner hook ≤ 6 words.
